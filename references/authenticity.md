# Authenticity Evaluation

Treat authenticity as a documented relationship among a recipe, people, place, and time — not a single universal score.

## Dimensions

Assess each dimension as **strong**, **moderate**, **weak**, **conflicting**, or **unknown**:

1. **Provenance** — Named author/collector, edition, page, and traceable publication history.
2. **Community proximity** — Creator or institution connected to the represented community.
3. **Geographic specificity** — Locality is precise and supported rather than attached as marketing.
4. **Temporal fit** — Source date and recipe features fit the period being claimed.
5. **Linguistic evidence** — Original name, terminology, and translation choices are documented.
6. **Technique continuity** — Characteristic process survives beyond a generic ingredient list.
7. **Ingredient context** — Ingredients and substitutions fit local availability and historical trade.
8. **Independent corroboration** — Multiple sources do not merely copy the same ancestor.
9. **Genre/context** — Household, ceremonial, street, restaurant, diaspora, institutional, or famine cooking is identified.
10. **Access integrity** — The recipe itself was inspected rather than inferred from a snippet or index.

Apply labels to individual claims, not automatically to an entire dish or source. A dish can be "strong" on community proximity but "weak" on temporal fit.

## Confidence Labels

| Label | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **High** | Directly inspected, community-proximate evidence with independent corroboration and no major unresolved conflict. |
| **Moderate** | Credible inspected evidence with limited corroboration, access, locality, or period precision. |
| **Low** | Plausible lead dominated by snippets, aggregators, unclear copying, or unresolved contradictions. |
| **Indeterminate** | Evidence is insufficient to choose among competing claims. Do not force a verdict. |

## Comparison Rules

- **Cluster before comparing.** Group recipes by region, period, and social context before comparing ingredients. Comparing a 19th-century Ukrainian peasant borscht with a 1970s Brooklyn Jewish deli borscht without noting the genre difference produces noise.
- **Trace shared wording.** Detect copying by searching for identical phrases across sources. Repeated text is not independent confirmation — it may all derive from one ancestor source.
- **Avoid "oldest equals original."** Survival in print reflects literacy, publishing infrastructure, class, and colonial archives. The earliest written recipe is not necessarily the origin; it is the earliest documented version.
- **Diaspora authenticity is real.** Treat diaspora forms as authentic to their own communities when supported. Do not grade them only by homeland conformity. A Jewish diaspora borscht is not a "corrupted" Ukrainian borscht — it is an authentic expression of a different community.
- **Modern substitutions are substitutions, not fraud.** Describe them honestly: "sour cream replaced with yogurt in post-1960s American publications" rather than "inauthentic."
- **Preserve plurality.** When the record supports multiple living variants, do not collapse them into one "canonical" recipe. Report the range.
- **Separate descriptive from prescriptive authenticity.** Descriptive: "documented in this community at this time." Prescriptive: "the only correct version." The skill practices descriptive authenticity only.

## Authenticity Signals (for qualitative triage)

When filtering large result sets, apply these heuristics to rank candidates:

**Positive signals:**
- The author, collector, or contributing community has a documented relationship to the represented tradition.
- Publication place and distribution context are relevant to the claim.
- The source is close to the period being studied, while acknowledging archival survival bias.
- Independent sources describe the same technique.
- Original-language terminology and translation history are inspectable.
- The work has a traceable edition and publication history.
- Ingredients and methods fit documented local availability and practice.

**Caution signals:**
- Substitutions are presented as historical without support.
- A fusion or modernized compilation is used to prove an earlier tradition.
- The creator's relationship to the represented community is unknown or merely marketed.
- Editorial provenance and source citations are absent.
- Only one uncorroborated source is available.
- The result is an unattributed aggregator, crowd-sourced entry, snippet, or dataset row.

Do not mechanically total points. Record the evidence behind each signal and cross-check before assigning confidence.

## Recommended Report Structure

1. Research question and scope
2. Names, spellings, region, period, and context
3. Best evidence with full citations
4. Variant comparison (table when 3+ recipes differ materially)
5. Stable features and contested features
6. Authenticity assessment by dimension (the 10 dimensions above)
7. Practical transcription / adaptation / reconstruction, if requested
8. Confidence, access gaps, and further leads
